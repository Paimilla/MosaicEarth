Instrumental Comparison & Methodological Correlation:

Methodological GPR vs RIMFAX Comparison: Terrestrial GPR at Makgadikgadi operated with low-frequency rough-terrain antennas at 30 MHz (vertical resolution ~0.67 m) and 50 MHz (~0.40 m), with an estimated radar velocity v ≈ 0.085 m/ns. On Mars, RIMFAX (FMCW 150–1200 MHz) recorded subsurface reflectors at ~15 m depth in the delta front (Sols 134–204) and at ~20 m with a ~12° structural dip and dielectric permittivity transition (ε_r ≈ 9 → 7) in the Séítah formation (Sols 400–566). A structural and dielectric contrast analogy is established (saline/clay-rich versus volcanic strata), ruling out direct metric depth matches.

Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) Methodological Framework

To evaluate which locations on Earth are viable analogs for permanent lunar bases and Mars missions, MOSAIC employs a transparent and explainable multi-criteria analysis model, avoiding unverifiable "black box" approaches.

Key Principle: Mission-Profile-Specific Evaluation

Not all dimensions are applicable to every planetary profile. For instance, a basaltic lava tube should not be penalized for failing to reproduce the Martian atmosphere, since its purpose is to test shielding and structural integrity in subsurface cavities. Similarly, a polar Antarctic base (Amundsen-Scott) is an operational analog for life support and heavy logistics, so its mineralogical similarity receives a minimal weight (w_geology = 0.2) to reflect its true operational value.
